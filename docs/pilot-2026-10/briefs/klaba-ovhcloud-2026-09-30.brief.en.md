# Audition of Mr Octave Klaba, CEO of OVHcloud (Economic Affairs Committee)
*2026-09-30 · transcript 00:05:00–01:25:00 · automatic analysis, machine-translated from French, verify before quoting*

## Summary
Mr. Octave Klaba, Chairman and CEO of OVHcloud, was heard before the Committee on Economic Affairs. He presented his company's strategy to reach 2 billion euros in turnover through global diversification and a critical mass necessary for investments. The main topics discussed were European digital sovereignty, public procurement as a lever to support European cloud actors, the Secnum Cloud qualification, infrastructure resilience (notably via water cooling and data center redundancy), dependence on American and Chinese hyperscalers, regulatory barriers to building large data centers in Europe, risks related to Chinese open source for AI, and anticompetitive practices by hyperscalers (tied licenses, free cloud credits, prohibitive egress fees). The tone was both defensive and offensive: Klaba defended OVHcloud's ability to contribute to European sovereignty while criticizing market imbalances and calling for political courage to redirect public budgets toward European providers.

## Key takeaways
- OVHcloud aims for a turnover of 2 billion euros through global diversification and a critical mass necessary for investments.
- Mr. Klaba proposes redirecting 5 to 15% of digital public procurement toward European actors to create a critical mass without relying solely on France.
- OVHcloud has used water cooling since 2004, which allows it to halve its electricity consumption.
- No data center of several gigawatts will be able to exist in Europe due to regulatory blockages and excessively long authorization delays, according to Mr. Klaba.
- Europe depends on Chinese open source for AI, which creates a strategic risk because the future intentions of these actors are unknown.
- One megawatt of capacity in a data center generates between 15 and 20 million euros in annual turnover for OVHcloud.
- OVHcloud has been working since 2004 to recover heat from its data centers for urban heating, despite current technical limitations.
- OVHcloud isolates its American activities in a dedicated subsidiary to comply with European digital sovereignty requirements.

## Position on AI
Mr. Octave Klaba is skeptical about calls for a pause in AI development, seeing them as disguised deregulation attempts rather than a genuine security requirement. He stresses that AI can chain low-impact security flaws to penetrate systems, exploiting very weak signals that have remained under the radar. He advocates a European approach to AI based on supply chain mastery, energy efficiency, and developing European capabilities to reduce dependence on hyperscalers. OVHcloud is developing an AI strategy across five levels, going beyond GPUs to cover the entire technological stack, investing in sustainable AI products. MPs, notably Mr. René Pilato (LFI) and Ms. Valérie Rossier (PS), questioned on infrastructure resilience and the place of SMEs in digital sovereignty, showing a shared concern about the risks of dependence on American and Chinese giants in AI.

## Open questions
- How can we ensure that the redirection of 5 to 15% of public procurement toward European actors will actually be implemented and respected?
- What concrete measures can be taken to lift the regulatory blockages preventing the construction of very large data centers in Europe?
- How can Europe reduce its dependence on Chinese open source for AI without compromising innovation?
- What regulatory framework could prevent anticompetitive practices by hyperscalers (tied licensing, prohibitive exit fees) while fostering innovation?
- How can rural SMEs be effectively supported in accessing sovereign and secure cloud solutions?
- What balance must be struck between supporting European cloud champions and preserving a diverse SME ecosystem, without replacing American giants with European ones?
