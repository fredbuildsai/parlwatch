# Audition of Mr Arthur Mensch, Mistral AI (commission of inquiry into digital dependencies and vulnerabilities)
*2026-05-12 · transcript 00:08:00–01:30:20 · automatic analysis, machine-translated from French, verify before quoting*

## Summary
Mr. Arthur Mensch, co-founder and CEO of Mistral AI, was heard by the inquiry commission on structural dependencies and systemic vulnerabilities in the digital sector. He presented his vision of AI as a lever for digital sovereignty for Europe, emphasizing the importance of European talent, open source, French energy, and public demand to reduce dependence on non-European actors. He detailed Mistral AI's economic model based on tokens, highlighted digital sovereignty issues, criticized European regulatory fragmentation, and called for a unified, aggressive European industrial strategy based on economic leverage rather than isolationism. The tone of the hearing was mainly constructive and strategic, mixing concerns about energy and technological dependencies, calls for investment and adapted regulation, and defense of an open and sovereign approach to AI in Europe.

## Key takeaways
- Mr. Arthur Mensch states that if Europe only provides energy for AI, it will lose 90% of the value created.
- Mistral AI is investing one billion euros this year in R&D to maintain its position as a leader in open models.
- Public procurement represents about 20% of Mistral AI's turnover, of which 10% comes from French public procurement.
- Nuclear electricity at 70% in France considerably reduces the carbon footprint of data centers built in France.
- Mr. Arthur Mensch criticizes the heaviness and fragmentation of regulations in Europe, which penalize startups and favor large companies.

## Position on AI
Mr. Arthur Mensch positions Mistral AI as a European actor defending open, sovereign AI strategically aligned with Europe's industrial and energy interests, while remaining open to foreign investments under governance control. He argues that AI must be treated as a strategic resource comparable to energy, intrinsically linked to the cloud, and developed with low-carbon energy, notably nuclear. MPs, notably Mr. Arnaud Saint-Martin, share concerns about digital sovereignty and risks of dependence on foreign actors, while questioning environmental impacts and balances between foreign capital attractiveness and value preservation in France. Some unknown MPs stress the need to combine AI and electrical capacity to regain a sustainable market share and warn of the risk of technological vassalage without strengthening independence.

## Open questions
